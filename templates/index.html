Honey traced

India leads the world in honey, and in honey fraud.

  • Sugar syrup slips past ordinary purity tests, so adulterated honey undercuts honest beekeepers.
  • Shoppers cannot tell raw single-origin honey from a blended import.
  • Paper records break the chain of custody between the hive, the processor, and the shelf.
  • Small apiaries earn less because they cannot prove where their honey came from.

One record per batch that anyone can check.

  • Every step is hashed and linked to the one before it. Alter a past record and the chain breaks where it changed.
  • Lab results for moisture, pollen, and C4 sugar stay pinned to the batch.
  • A shopper scans the jar and reads the origin, the floral source, and the full journey.
  • Beekeepers charge more once they can prove a pure, single-origin harvest.

What a honey supply chain needs, in one system.

The same record serves beekeepers, processors, retailers, and the person holding the jar.

Tamper-evident ledger

Each step is a SHA-256 block linked to the last. One click re-hashes the chain and shows whether anything changed.

QR on every jar

The code opens the batch's full record in a browser. No app to install.

Hive register

Record each hive with its floral source and location, then track harvests and yield per colony.

Pinned lab results

Moisture, pollen count, and adulteration tests are written into the block and cannot be edited afterwards.

Chain explorer

Read the raw chain: indices, nonces, and previous-hash links, the way a public block explorer shows them.

JSON API

Partners and scanners fetch a batch's verified history from one REST endpoint.

From hive to shelf in four steps.

01

02

04
